Cash flow is measured against not installing solar: electricity savings and rebates are inflows; purchases and loan payments are outflows. The investment-adjusted view subtracts the after-tax investment growth forgone by a cash purchase, without treating that growth as spendable monthly income.

Important client disclosures

Financing does not necessarily make solar cash-flow positive. A 5 kW array at $0.11/kWh saves about $45.83/month in year one, before any rate changes. A $116.20 loan payment produces an initial monthly shortfall of about $70.37, before considering the rebate. The rebate is a one-time inflow, not recurring monthly savings.

Refinancing is not guaranteed. Eligibility, interest rates, closing costs and available home equity can change. The 14% reset scenario assumes the same monthly payment, which can cause negative amortization if the payment is below monthly interest. Confirm Financeit's actual contract terms, payment recalculation and prepayment rules before presenting a quote.

Rebates are conditional. $5,000 and month 2 are adjustable sales assumptions, not a promise of eligibility, approval or payment timing. Utility rates, net-metering credits, consumption, taxes and site production affect realized savings.
